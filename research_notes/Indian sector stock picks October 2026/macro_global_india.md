**Big picture:** 2026 is a supply-side oil and energy shock year. A US/Israel–Iran war started on 28 Feb 2026, and the Strait of Hormuz has been closed or disrupted on and off since. Brent is around $110-117 in late October. The Fed and the RBI are both hiking, the rupee is near record lows (around 95-97/USD), FIIs are selling heavily, and Indian WPI is in double digits. This is closer to a 2022-style (or 2008-style) oil spike than to the 2014-16 crash. Analogies to the crash only apply as a *reversal* scenario (for example a Hormuz reopening or peace deal; EIA sees Brent at $84 in 2027).

## 1. Crude oil: price, trend, drivers, forecasts, India impact

### Takeaway
Brent is about $110-117/bbl in late Oct 2026. It peaked at a four-year high of about $126 earlier in 2026 and briefly fell below $80 in early August during a truce, before re-spiking on renewed Hormuz attacks and the Sept 2026 Saudi East-West pipeline strike. EIA (Oct 2026 STEO) forecasts $105 for 4Q26 and $84 average for 2027, so the consensus path is lower oil over 6-12 months, with large two-way risk. For India this means a heavy import bill, OMC under-recoveries, a weak rupee and double-digit WPI.

### Inferences
- The baseline (EIA $105 for 4Q26, then $84 for 2027) means crude falls about 25% over 12 months. That favours a gradual rotation toward oil-consuming sectors (paints, OMCs, aviation, tyres, chemicals) in 2027, but timing depends on Hormuz diplomacy. A fresh escalation (Bab el-Mandeb, Saudi infrastructure) is the main upside tail.
- Assuming India imports about 4.5–5 mb/d (my estimate, not sourced), a $10/bbl change in crude is roughly $16–18bn a year on the import bill.

## 6. Historical analogies: how similar episodes moved Indian sectors

### Takeaway
Searches turned up few sourced, quantified sector returns for past episodes. Directional evidence is consistent: OMCs, paints, aviation and tyres rally when crude falls and drop when crude spikes, while upstream (ONGC, Oil India) does the opposite. The 2013 taper tantrum saw the rupee fall about 17–19% (to about 68.8 on 28 Aug 2013) and emergency RBI tightening. That is a close policy analogue to Oct 2026, when the RBI hiked and imposed FX curbs.

### Cited Findings
- **2013:** The rupee hit a record low of about 68.85 intraday on 28 Aug 2013 (sources vary from 68.36 to 68.85), down about 17–19% from about 55.6 in May. RBI raised MSF/emergency rates by 200 bp on 15 Jul 2013. The FCNR(B) swap window raised about $26bn — [HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/sky-learn/market-wtf-by-sky/how-the-2013-taper-tantrum-hit-indian-markets-hardest); [Medium (Harsh Gupta)](https://harshvgupta.medium.com/rbis-response-to-the-taper-tantrum-crisis-of-2013-ec934257f8f9); [RiskHub](https://riskhub.org/blogs/the-taper-tantrum-of-2013)
- **2015 crude crash:** In Jan 2015 Asian Paints hit a record high (+~4% on the day) as oil fell to a nearly six-year low — [Business Standard Jan 2015](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/asian-paints-touches-new-high-as-oil-slump-115011300133_1.html)
- **Recent pattern:**
  - When Brent nears $100–110, OMCs (BPCL, HPCL down more than 3%), aviation, paints and tyres fall, and Reliance and IndiGo tumble — [HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/bpcl-hpcl-slide-over-3-percent); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/stocks/reliance-hpcl-bpcl-indigo-asian-paints-indian-oil-oil-sensitive-stocks-tumble-as-crude-oil-prices-jump-to-near-110-barrel/article-200159/)
  - When oil fell 5% on the Iran–Israel ceasefire (24 Jun 2025), OMCs, paints, tyres and aviation rallied — [Business Standard Jun 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/hpcl-bpcl-ioc-asian-paints-indigo-omc-paint-tyre-aviation-stocks-rally-crude-oil-slip-us-president-donald-trump-iran-israel-ceasefire-125062400248_1.html); [Ventura](https://www.venturasecurities.com/news/stocks/indigo-bpcl-hpcl-lead-rally-as-oil-sensitive-stocks-gain-on-crude-crash/)
  - Goldman (Mar 2026) upgraded upstream and downgraded OMCs during the spike — [roundup](https://www.equityresearchindia.com/post/foreign-brokerages-india-market-outlook-2026-to-2027-a-roundup-of-morgan-stanley-goldman-sachs-an)
- **Diverging intraday reaction:** Some days, OMCs gain while paints, tyres and chemicals fall on a 2% crude drop, which shows the two groups don't always move together intraday — [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/stocks/bpcl-hpcl-and-ioc-gain-while-paints-tyres-and-chemical-stocks-fall-as-crude-oil-prices-drop-2/article-202368/)

### Inferences
- Unverified background from general knowledge, not sourced in this session. These are approximate and must be checked before use:
  - **2014–16:** Brent fell from about $115 (Jun 2014) to about $27–28 (Jan 2016). Over that window Asian Paints, Berger, Pidilite, HPCL and BPCL re-rated strongly (OMCs also gained from diesel deregulation in Oct 2014). IndiGo listed in Nov 2015, after much of the fall. ONGC and Oil India underperformed.
  - **2022:** Brent briefly hit about $139 (Mar 2022). OMCs posted large H1 FY23 losses as retail prices were frozen. Paints and aviation corrected. Upstream gained until the windfall tax (Jul 2022).
  - **2020:** WTI briefly went negative (Apr 2020). Recovery led by IT, pharma and metals.
  - **2013:** IT and pharma exporters outperformed during the rupee slide.
- The closest analogue for Oct 2026 is **2022**: a war-driven supply shock, administered fuel prices, OMC under-recoveries, central banks hiking and FII selling. Then, OMC and paint stocks bottomed *before* crude peaked, and re-rated once crude rolled over. The **2013** episode matches the rupee and RBI-response dimension.

## 7. Sector impact matrix (current macro: Brent ~$110–117, INR ~95–97, RBI and Fed hiking, CPI 5.5%, WPI 11.4%, weak monsoon, GDP 7–8%)

### Takeaway
**Negative near term:**
- OMCs: under-recoveries, though improving in Q2.
- Paints, aviation, tyres, chemicals: crude-linked input costs.
- FMCG (rural): weak monsoon and palm costs.
- Realty and rate-sensitives: rate hikes.
- Fertiliser and gas utilities: LNG shortage.

**Positive near term:**
- Upstream oil (ONGC, Oil India): high realisations, subject to windfall-tax or subsidy-sharing risk.
- IT and pharma exporters: rupee near 95–97, though US demand is "modest".
- Non-ferrous metals: copper and aluminium near records.
- Defence and power/infra: domestic capex, GFCF +11.9%.

**Neutral to positive:** banks (NIM support from hikes vs credit-cost risk), autos (GST 2.0 and festive demand vs fuel and input costs).

On a 6–12 month view, if EIA's $84 Brent for 2027 plays out, the oil-consumer losers become the biggest rebound candidates.
