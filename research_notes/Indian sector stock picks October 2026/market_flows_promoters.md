## 2. Investor flows: FPI, DII, mutual funds and SIPs, retail, F&O, IPOs

### Takeaway
2026 is a record year of FPI selling in secondary markets, at about ₹3.0-3.1 lakh crore by mid-October per NSDL and roughly double 2025's ₹1.66 lakh crore. DIIs and SIPs have more than absorbed it: DII buying is about ₹6.8-7.0 lakh crore YTD and SIPs hit a record ₹32,490 crore in September. FPIs are still net buyers in primary markets, so the IPO pipeline remains hot (Jio Platforms raised ₹30,213 crore and listed +21%). Retail F&O participation fell after SEBI curbs.

### Inferences
- Domestic money (SIPs above ₹32k crore/month plus insurance and EPFO) is the market's structural floor. FPIs are the marginal seller, and their selling is driven by global yields and oil, not by Indian corporate failure. Signs of a turn would be US 10-year yields easing below 5%, crude falling, or MSCI weight stabilising.
- FPIs selling listed stocks while buying IPOs is a "sell old, buy new and cheap" pattern. Primary issuance (Jio) is also absorbing domestic liquidity that might otherwise support the secondary market.

## 4. Promoter activity and governance: real vs fake signals

### Takeaway
Credible 2026 promoter-confidence signals include Reliance (creeping market buying to 50.48%), Thomas Cook (promoter bought about 4.4 million shares in June), and smaller buys at Eveready, Alembic Pharma and GRM Overseas. Mixed or red-flag cases include Granules India, where the promoter sold 1.72 crore shares in September 2026 while promoter-group warrants were converted. SEBI enforcement on pump-and-dump schemes continues (a 222-entity order). Auditor resignations in October 2026 were mostly fee or capacity related, but each needs scrutiny.

### Cited Findings
**Promoter buying (bullish when funded by cash and done in the open market)**
- Reliance Industries: promoter stake rose about 0.5 percentage points in Q1 FY27 to 50.48% (30 Jun 2026) through market purchases of about ₹8,500-9,000 crore. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/reliance-promoters-raise-stake-to-5048-through-market-purchases-signalling-long-term-growth-confidence); [Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/bankingfinance/Reliance-Promoters-Raise-Stake-to-5048percent-in-Q1-FY26/6a59da2efe1c9287f472f1ce)
- Thomas Cook (India): promoter entity Fairbridge Capital Mauritius bought about 4.4 million shares in early June 2026, taking its holding to about 64.76%. Promoter buying was also reported at GRM Overseas, Eveready Industries and Alembic Pharmaceuticals. NCC Ltd showed both stake increases and pledge activity. — [Tradebrains](https://tradebrains.in/5-stocks-where-promoters-increased-stakes-and-pledged-shares/)

### Inferences: real vs fake signal checklist (synthesised from the above and standard practice)
- **Genuine confidence:**
  - Open-market purchases by promoters using their own cash (e.g. Reliance, Thomas Cook), especially during drawdowns.
  - Rising promoter stake without new pledges.
  - Insiders (MD, CFO) buying.
  - Buying by MFs and DIIs alongside the promoter (Reliance in September).
  - Operating cash flow at least about 80% of PAT.
  - Auditor stable and from a reputable firm.
- **Ambiguous:**
  - Stake increases via preferential warrants priced below market, or a creeping buy that coincides with pledging (NCC). Warrant conversion (Granules) can raise stake percentages without new cash at market prices.
  - A large block sale near the same time, as at Granules, contradicts the bullish reading.
- **Red flags:**
  - Rising pledge percentage, especially if pledged shares exceed about 20-25% of promoter holding, or pledges added in a falling market (margin-call risk).
  - Promoter OFS or block sales during euphoria.
  - Large or growing related-party transactions; receivables and "other assets" growing faster than sales.
  - PAT not converting to cash.
  - Mid-term auditor exit, particularly with vague reasons ("pre-occupations") rather than documented fee disputes.
  - SME or illiquid stocks with sudden volume spikes, Telegram/WhatsApp tips, or many related entities trading among themselves (Mauria Udyog pattern).
  - IPO proceeds routed as "issue expenses" (Varyaa).
  - Frequent preferential allotments to unknown entities.
